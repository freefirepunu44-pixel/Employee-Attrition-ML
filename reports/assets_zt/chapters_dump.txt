INTRODUCTION
P5 [Normal]: 1.1 BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
P6 [Normal]: Digital identity management is a foundational component of modern online infrastructure. Every digital interaction—accessing banking services, submitting academic certificates, seeking enterprise resource access—relies on some mechanism for asserting and validating identity. The dominant paradigm today is the perimeter-based centralized model, in which boundary firewalls and VPNs authenticate users once and implicitly trust subsequent activities. While this model has been operationally convenient, it harbours serious structural vulnerabilities that have become increasingly apparent in the context of contemporary large-scale data breaches and lateral movement attacks.
P7 [Normal]: According to the Identity Theft Resource Center, the year 2023 alone witnessed over 2,116 data compromises affecting more than 343 million individuals globally. In corporate and institutional domains, compromised credentials have become the single leading initial attack vector, with an average dwell time of over 200 days before initial containment. Centralized single-gate security architectures represent single points of failure: once perimeter defenses are penetrated, adversaries exploit internal trust to traverse internal subnets, exfiltrate confidential databases, and execute ransomware attacks.
P8 [Normal]: The emergence of the Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) paradigm, formalized through NIST SP 800-207, provides an opportunity to fundamentally re-architect digital security. Zero Trust rejects implicit trust based on network topology, enforcing continuous contextual verification under the principle "Never Trust, Always Verify." Integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) enables automated multi-signal risk estimation and behavioral anomaly detection, creating adaptive security barriers capable of defending against modern credential misuse in real time.
P9 [Normal]: AI-ZeroTrust has been conceived and developed to synthesize these advanced technologies into an authentic, production-grade security platform. The system addresses critical gaps in conventional access gateways by incorporating a 16-control verification engine, dual-AI risk regression and anomaly detection, real-time SMTP Email OTP, passwordless 128D facial biometrics, and an immutable SHA-256 cryptographic audit ledger.
P10 [Normal]: 1.2 PROBLEM STATEMENT
P11 [Normal]: Centralized perimeter access systems suffer from severe and well-documented structural vulnerabilities:
P12 [Normal]: • Implicit Internal Trust: Once credentials authenticate past boundary firewalls, internal network resources remain completely unprotected against lateral traversal.
P13 [Normal]: • Static Rule Limitations: Conventional Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) evaluates static permissions, failing to detect anomalous logins occurring at off-hours or from suspicious IP addresses.
P14 [Normal]: • Credential Forgery & Hijacking: Password-only authentication remains vulnerable to credential stuffing, session token replay, and phishing.
P15 [Normal]: • Absence of Cryptographic Auditability: Standard access loggers lack forward tamper-evident chaining, allowing malicious insiders to alter or delete access records without detection.

P37 [Normal]: CHAPTER 2
LITERATURE SURVEY
P38 [Normal]: 2.1 CENTRALIZED AND PERIMETER DEFENSE SYSTEMS
P39 [Normal]: Traditional network architectures rely on border defenses such as firewalls and VPN concentrators to filter traffic entering corporate intranets. Anderson et al. [1] demonstrated that perimeter-focused security creates an artificial divide: once attackers compromise boundary credentials, they enter an unmonitored flat internal network where servers and workstations communicate freely. The 2017 Equifax breach (147 million records) and 2021 LinkedIn exposure (700 million records) empirically confirmed the catastrophic risk of single-gate centralized access.
P40 [Normal]: 2.2 NIST ZERO TRUST ARCHITECTURE STANDARDS
P41 [Normal]: NIST Special Publication 800-207 [2] formalized the core tenets of Zero Trust: all data sources are resources, communications are secured regardless of location, access is granted per session, and authentication/authorization are dynamic. NIST SP 1800-35 [3] provided deployment blueprints for federal and enterprise adoption, establishing the decoupling of Policy Enforcement Points (PEP) and Policy Decision Points (PDP).
P42 [Normal]: 2.3 GOOGLE BEYONDCORP INITIATIVE
P43 [Normal]: Google pioneered perimeterless corporate security with BeyondCorp [4], migrating all internal applications to an Access Proxy that validates user identities and managed device certificates. While BeyondCorp eliminated corporate VPNs, it relies heavily on proprietary device agents, making adoption challenging in heterogeneous environments with personal or contractor devices.
P44 [Normal]: 2.4 MACHINE LEARNING IN DYNAMIC ACCESS CONTROL
P45 [Normal]: Al-Daeef et al. [5] demonstrated that supervised machine learning analyzing contextual telemetry significantly outperforms static access rules. Furthermore, Liu et al. [8] proved the efficacy of Isolation Forests for identifying behavioral outliers without requiring labeled attack datasets.

SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
P51 [Normal]: 3.1 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
P52 [Normal]: 3.1.1 Authentication & Enrollment Module
P53 [Normal]: • User Registration: Allows user registration with PBKDF2-SHA256 password hashing and mandatory email verification.
P54 [Normal]: • Live Email OTP: Generates single-use 6-digit OTP tokens delivered via SMTP (15-min TTL, 60s resend cooldown).
P55 [Normal]: • Facial Enrollment: Captures 128D facial feature vectors, encrypts them via Fernet AES-128, and binds them to the user record.
P56 [Normal]: 3.1.2 Telemetry & Context Verification Module
P57 [Normal]: • 18 Signals: Harvests hardware, environmental, network, and temporal telemetry on every request.
P58 [Normal]: • 16 Controls: Executes 16 structured checks returning status, confidence rating, and limitation provenance.
P59 [Normal]: 3.1.3 Policy Enforcement & Decision Module
P60 [Normal]: • Dual-AI Engine: Computes continuous risk scores (0–100) and detects behavioral outlier drift.
P61 [Normal]: • Dynamic Policies: Enforces policies ZT-POL-001 through 005 returning ALLOW, STEP_UP_AUTHENTICATION, DENY, or BLOCK.
P62 [Normal]: 3.1.4 Audit & Administration Module
P63 [Normal]: • SHA-256 Ledger: Commits all access decisions to an immutable forward-chained cryptographic ledger.
P64 [Normal]: • Session Revocation: Empowers Level-4 Security Officers to terminate sessions in 1-click via token_blocklist.
P65 [Normal]: 3.2 NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
P66 [Normal]: • Performance: Full policy evaluation executes under 350 ms (~249 ms average).
P67 [Normal]: • Security: Zero plaintext password or biometric storage; all tokens stored in memory.
P68 [Normal]: • Tamper Resistance: Historical log alterations break the forward SHA-256 chain, triggering automated alert detection.

P76 [Normal]: 4.1 HIGH-LEVEL ARCHITECTURE
P77 [Normal]: The AI-ZeroTrust platform is architected around four decoupled layers adhering to NIST SP 800-207:
P78 [Normal]: • 1. Client & Telemetry Harvester: Browser-based ES6 modules capturing 18 parameters without third-party frameworks.
P79 [Normal]: • 2. Policy Enforcement Point (PEP): Flask REST gateway intercepting all inbound requests, applying rate limiting and CSRF defense.
P80 [Normal]: • 3. Policy Decision Point (PDP): Dual-AI analytical engine computing continuous risk scores and rendering policy verdicts.
P81 [Normal]: • 4. Cryptographic Storage Layer: SQLite WAL database storing encrypted biometrics and the forward-chained SHA-256 audit ledger.

P106 [Normal]: CHAPTER 5
METHODOLOGY / SYSTEM DESIGN
P107 [Normal]: 5.1 16-CONTROL VERIFICATION ENGINE
P108 [Normal]: Before machine learning evaluation occurs, the inbound request is processed through the VerificationEngine (ztrust/services/verification.py). The engine executes exactly 16 structured checks, each returning a strongly-typed VerificationCheck object with explicit status, confidence score, source provenance, and limitations.
P109 [Normal]: • Identity & Email Verification: Validates JWT signature, active account state, and confirmed email record in database.
P110 [Normal]: • MFA Status: Validates enrolled facial vector, email OTP, or TOTP secrets.
P111 [Normal]: • Device & Browser Posture: Evaluates hardware attributes, user-agent integrity, and headless browser flags.
P112 [Normal]: • Geographic & Velocity Checks: Calculates physical travel speed between consecutive logins to detect impossible travel.
P113 [Normal]: • Transport & Session Encryption: Validates TLS transport, secure HttpOnly cookie flags, and active token blocklist state.

P130 [Normal]: 5.5 FORWARD-CHAINED SHA-256 AUDIT LEDGER
P131 [Normal]: Every access event is committed to an immutable forward-chained cryptographic ledger. Each log digest is computed recursively:
P132 [Normal]: H_i = SHA-256( Event_ID || Timestamp || User_ID || Action || Result || H_{i-1} )
P133 [Normal]: Where H_{i-1} represents the hash digest of the immediate predecessor. Any retroactive modification breaks the chain from that block forward, enabling automated tampering detection.
