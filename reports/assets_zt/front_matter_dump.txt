P80 [Body Text] (align=JUSTIFY (3)): AI-ZeroTrust is an enterprise-grade, continuous context-verification Zero Trust platform that enforces strict "Never Trust, Always Verify" governance adhering to NIST SP 800-207. The architecture bifurcates enforcement into a Policy Enforcement Point (PEP) and an intelligent Policy Decision Point (PDP). The system extracts 18 contextual telemetry signals across device posture, IP reputation, physical travel velocity, off-hours anomalies, and network anonymizers. A dual-AI risk decision engine combines a supervised Random Forest Regressor (model RF-2026-v4) predicting continuous risk scores between 0 and 100 with an unsupervised Isolation Forest model detecting behavioral drift. The Policy Decision Point evaluates five database-backed security policies (ZT-POL-001 through ZT-POL-005) returning typed verdicts: ALLOW, STEP_UP_AUTHENTICATION, DENY, or BLOCK. Multi-factor identity assurance is fortified using real-time SMTP Email OTP transmission and standalone 128-dimensional facial biometric vector matching featuring OpenCV Haar-Cascade liveness anti-spoofing. All authentication events, access verdicts, and administrative session revocations are committed to an immutable, forward-chained SHA-256 audit ledger with real-time cryptographic integrity verification. A comprehensive test suite comprising 63 automated integration tests validates cryptographic correctness, access control, and system resilience with a 100% pass rate. The Random Forest model achieves an R² score of 0.9849, Mean Absolute Error of 2.16, and an average decision latency of 249.29 ms, demonstrating production-grade viability.

INTRODUCTION
P111 [Normal] (align=LEFT (0)): 1.1 BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
P112 [Normal] (align=JUSTIFY (3)): Digital identity management is a foundational component of modern online infrastructure. Every digital interaction—accessing banking services, submitting academic certificates, seeking enterprise resource access—relies on some mechanism for asserting and validating identity. The dominant paradigm today is the perimeter-based centralized model, in which boundary firewalls and VPNs authenticate users once and implicitly trust subsequent activities. While this model has been operationally convenient, it harbours serious structural vulnerabilities that have become increasingly apparent in the context of contemporary large-scale data breaches and lateral movement attacks.
P113 [Normal] (align=JUSTIFY (3)): According to the Identity Theft Resource Center, the year 2023 alone witnessed over 2,116 data compromises affecting more than 343 million individuals globally. In corporate and institutional domains, compromised credentials have become the single leading initial attack vector, with an average dwell time of over 200 days before initial containment. Centralized single-gate security architectures represent single points of failure: once perimeter defenses are penetrated, adversaries exploit internal trust to traverse internal subnets, exfiltrate confidential databases, and execute ransomware attacks.
P114 [Normal] (align=JUSTIFY (3)): The emergence of the Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) paradigm, formalized through NIST SP 800-207, provides an opportunity to fundamentally re-architect digital security. Zero Trust rejects implicit trust based on network topology, enforcing continuous contextual verification under the principle "Never Trust, Always Verify." Integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) enables automated multi-signal risk estimation and behavioral anomaly detection, creating adaptive security barriers capable of defending against modern credential misuse in real time.
